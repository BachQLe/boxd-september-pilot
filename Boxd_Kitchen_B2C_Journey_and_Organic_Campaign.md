# Box’d Kitchen UVA — B2C Journey Audit + One-Month Organic Campaign

Prepared for the first Box’d Kitchen growth pilot. September 2026.

## Executive call

Box’d does not have a demand problem so much as a journey problem. The restaurant has credible food, strong ratings, UVA social proof, direct pickup and delivery, and clear hero products. What it lacks is one owned path that turns a specific piece of content into a measurable order.

The pilot should not treat “organic content” as the outcome. It should test three distinct reasons a UVA customer might choose Box’d:

1. **Rational proof:** Box’d is the practical, obvious next meal.
2. **Campus identity:** Box’d is a UVA spot people like me already choose.
3. **Product utility:** Box’d makes it easy to find an order I will crave.

Each reason gets its own recurring content set, landing page, URL, campaign tag, and—if the owner approves—unique Toast offer code. This creates the beginnings of a real B2C journey rather than a month of disconnected Reels.

## Current journey audit

### What exists now

| Journey stage | Current experience | What is working | Main gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Instagram, creator collaborations, athlete/customer posts, Google and delivery marketplaces | Recent UVA football/creator content creates strong local relevance | Content does not consistently hand the viewer one clear next action |
| Consideration | Instagram profile and posts, third-party menu pages, ratings | “Growing with UVA since 2018,” student-powered positioning, recognizable bowls, strong marketplace ratings | No owned place that organizes the value proposition or helps a new customer choose |
| Conversion | Toast direct ordering, Uber Eats/Postmates, DoorDash, Grubhub, Elevate, in-store kiosk | Pickup and delivery are available; Toast advertises no platform service fee | Too many endpoints; no campaign-specific link or clean attribution from organic post to order |
| Experience | Dine-in, pickup and delivery at 909 W Main St | Simple menu, clear hero proteins, portable format | Menu language and availability vary by platform; some item copy contains typos and inconsistencies |
| Retention | Elevate loyalty, occasional discounts, gift cards, organic social | Existing repeat-customer behavior and a documented Elevate reward | Retention is outsourced and mostly invisible in the owned social journey |
| Advocacy | Student-athlete features, regular-customer stories, family/community moments | Box’d has unusually authentic UVA proof for a local restaurant | Advocacy is posted as content, but not systematically captured and reused as conversion proof |

### Strongest assets to exploit

- Box’d has served the UVA community since 2018 and describes the Charlottesville account as powered by UVA students.
- Recent content includes UVA quarterback Cole Geer identifying Box’d as a favorite place to eat.
- Uber Eats/Postmates shows a 4.8 rating from 1,000+ ratings.
- Elevate lists Box’d at 4.95 and offers pickup, delivery and a repeat-purchase reward.
- Chicken, lamb and steak over rice are clear hero products. The menu is small enough to make choosing easy.
- Toast is the best default outbound ordering destination because it supports pickup and delivery and advertises no platform service fee.

### Highest-priority fixes before launch

1. Make the Instagram bio promise one thing and give one link: **“Find your Box’d order ↓”**
2. Use a simple campaign hub with three page choices only during the test; each post should deep-link directly to its matching page whenever the platform allows.
3. Add GA4 or another first-party analytics tag to the landing pages before the first post.
4. Configure three unique Toast promo codes only if the owner approves a discount: `WHYBOXD`, `HOOSBOXD`, and `MYBOXD`. Keep the discount identical so the message—not the offer—is the test.
5. Standardize menu names, prices, descriptions, imagery and availability across Toast, Uber Eats, DoorDash, Grubhub and Elevate.
6. Ask Toast whether completed orders can be segmented by promo code, referral source or campaign parameters. Clicks alone are not revenue attribution.

## Pilot design

### Audience

Primary: UVA undergraduates who live, study or socialize near Central Grounds and the Corner, especially students deciding on a fast lunch or dinner.

Secondary: UVA student-athletes, student organizations and local young professionals who can become repeat buyers or group-order advocates.

### Conversion goal

The primary conversion is a click from a campaign landing page to direct Toast ordering. The true business outcome is a completed direct order or validated in-store redemption. Do not call views or landing-page traffic conversions.

### Test structure

| Set | Customer belief tested | Landing page | Organic CTA | Primary measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Five Reasons | “Box’d is the smartest/easiest next meal.” | `/five-reasons/` | “Tap the link for all five.” | Order clicks per landing-page session |
| 2. UVA Eats Here | “People like me already trust this place.” | `/uva-favorite/` | “See why UVA keeps coming back.” | Order clicks plus saves/shares |
| 3. Find Your Box | “I already know what I want from Box’d.” | `/find-your-box/` | “Find your order in 15 seconds.” | Chooser completion and order clicks |

Keep posting volume, days, offer and production quality as even as possible across sets. Otherwise the comparison will be contaminated.

## Content Set 1 — Five Reasons

**Job:** Acquire people who have seen Box’d but never formed a reason to try it.

**Style:** Direct-to-camera opening, hard on-screen typography, fast food inserts, numbered structure, 18–28 seconds.

**Recurring hook language:**

- “Top five reasons UVA students choose Box’d—and number five is the real reason.”
- “If you’re hungry near UVA, here are five reasons you’re overthinking lunch.”
- “Three things Box’d gives you that your sad dining-hall plate does not.”
- “Do not order Box’d until you know which protein actually fits you.”

### Four-post run

1. **Top five reasons to choose Box’d**  
   Hook: “Top five reasons your next UVA meal should be Box’d.”  
   Blocks: full-meal visual; chicken/lamb/steak choice; 909 W Main proximity; direct pickup/delivery; white-sauce payoff.  
   CTA: “All five—and the menu—are in the link.”

2. **The $15 test**  
   Hook: “What does about $15 actually get you near UVA?”  
   Blocks: unopened box; reveal; protein close-up; rice/salad/pita; empty-box or satisfied-customer payoff.  
   CTA: “See the full first-order case in our bio.”

3. **Bowl anatomy**  
   Hook: “This is why Box’d does not eat like another sad grain bowl.”  
   Blocks: rice base; salad color; protein; white sauce; hot sauce; pita.  
   CTA: “Tap to see which order to start with.”

4. **The class-gap challenge**  
   Hook: “Can you get a real meal between classes without wasting the whole gap?”  
   Blocks: walk-in timestamp; order; pickup; first bite; elapsed time.  
   CTA: “Order ahead from the five-reasons page.”

## Content Set 2 — UVA Eats Here

**Job:** Turn existing community credibility into trial and repeat behavior.

**Style:** Faces first, conversational, handheld footage, customer names/roles on screen, food as supporting evidence rather than the only subject.

**Recurring hook language:**

- “We asked UVA students for the Box’d order they would defend.”
- “The UVA regulars know something first-years do not.”
- “What does a UVA quarterback order at Box’d?”
- “Meet the people keeping this Charlottesville spot alive.”

### Four-post run

1. **The regular order**  
   Hook: “We asked five UVA regulars for the Box’d order they never switch.”  
   Blocks: name/year; order; why; bite; rapid scoreboard of most-mentioned protein.  
   CTA: “See why UVA eats here.”

2. **Athlete order remix**  
   Hook: “UVA QB Cole Geer called Box’d his favorite spot—so we tried his order.”  
   Blocks: reference clip/photo with permission; order build; student taste test; verdict.  
   CTA: “Join the regulars from the link.”

3. **Powered by UVA students**  
   Hook: “You have eaten here. But do you know who actually keeps Box’d moving?”  
   Blocks: two or three student employees; favorite menu item; one fast shift moment; team shot.  
   CTA: “Meet your next regular order.”

4. **Friend handoff**  
   Hook: “Send this to the person who still has not tried Box’d.”  
   Blocks: regular brings a first-timer; chooses order for them; first bite; honest rating.  
   CTA: “Bring one person this week.”

## Content Set 3 — Find Your Box

**Job:** Make the food visually craveable and remove menu indecision.

**Style:** Macro food shots, clean sound design, choice prompts, polls, bracket graphics and comment participation.

**Recurring hook language:**

- “Chicken, lamb or steak—you have three seconds.”
- “Your Box’d order based on one question.”
- “Bowl people and wrap people are fundamentally different.”
- “Choose your fighter: white sauce or hot sauce.”

### Four-post run

1. **Protein bracket**  
   Hook: “Chicken, lamb or steak: only one gets your next order.”  
   Blocks: identical beauty shot for each; quick taste descriptors; comment vote.  
   CTA: “Use the order finder in our bio.”

2. **Bowl vs. wrap**  
   Hook: “Bowl people and wrap people are not the same.”  
   Blocks: portability; portion; sauce distribution; student choice.  
   CTA: “Pick yours in 15 seconds.”

3. **Sauce scale**  
   Hook: “How much hot sauce can a UVA student actually handle?”  
   Blocks: 0–5 scale; reactions; winning ratio; white-sauce reset.  
   CTA: “Build the move from the link.”

4. **First-order prescription**  
   Hook: “Tell me your usual lunch and I’ll give you a Box’d order.”  
   Blocks: three student archetypes; recommendation for each; on-screen price; direct page screen recording.  
   CTA: “Find your own order in our bio.”

## Four-week publishing calendar

Post three primary Reels per week—one from each set—so every hypothesis receives four comparable executions. Use Stories to extend the test, not as separate creative concepts.

| Week | Tuesday | Thursday | Saturday | Story support |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Five Reasons | Regular Orders | Protein Bracket | Poll: “Have you tried Box’d?” plus reshare every tagged response |
| 2 | $15 Test | Athlete Order Remix | Bowl vs. Wrap | Price guess, protein vote, link sticker to matching page |
| 3 | Bowl Anatomy | Powered by UVA Students | Sauce Scale | Behind-the-scenes clips and question box for orders |
| 4 | Class-Gap Challenge | Friend Handoff | First-Order Prescription | Final vote, limited owner-approved offer, best-comments recap |

Recommended windows to test initially: 11:00 a.m.–12:00 p.m. for lunch-intent content and 4:30–6:00 p.m. for dinner-intent content. Keep each set’s day/time rotation balanced rather than assigning one set the strongest window every week.

## Production system

### One weekly shoot, 90 minutes

Capture enough modular footage for all three sets:

- Three clean hero builds: chicken, lamb and steak over rice.
- Bowl and wrap comparison.
- White sauce and hot sauce macro shots.
- Exterior/location and walk-in footage.
- Ordering, handoff and first-bite footage.
- Two staff mini-interviews.
- Three to five student/regular mini-interviews with written permission.
- One group/social moment.

Every interview should generate multiple hooks and standalone content blocks. Editors should preserve natural speech and avoid turning every clip into the same montage.

### Editing rules

- Show a face, finished bowl or explicit claim in the first 0.7 seconds.
- Put the spoken hook on screen; do not introduce the logo first.
- Change visual information every 1–2 seconds during the first five seconds.
- Use captions on every spoken clip.
- End with one action only. Never stack “follow, like, comment and order.”
- Match the CTA wording and destination to the content set.

## Landing pages

### Page 1: Five Reasons

Purpose: convert viewers who want a clear case for choosing Box’d.  
Hero: “5 reasons your next meal should be Box’d.”  
Core modules: five concise reasons, meal photography, direct-price proof, location, pickup/delivery, Toast CTA.  
Risk: generic list content can attract views without intent. The page must keep the order button visible on mobile.

### Page 2: UVA Eats Here

Purpose: convert viewers through community belonging and social proof.  
Hero: “UVA eats here.”  
Core modules: since-2018 story, student-powered claim, athlete/regular proof, location, Toast CTA.  
Risk: obtain permission before using a person’s image or quote in a commercial landing page. The current prototype uses text-only references.

### Page 3: Find Your Box

Purpose: turn food interest into a specific product decision.  
Hero: “Find your Box.”  
Core modules: protein choice, bowl/wrap choice, dynamic order recommendation, fallback first-order recommendation, Toast CTA.  
Risk: the current order button opens the general Toast menu. Phase two should deep-link recommendations to the exact item if Toast preserves item-level URLs reliably.

## Measurement plan

### Event taxonomy

| Event | Meaning | Required properties |
|---|---|---|
| `landing_view` | A visitor reached one of the three pages | campaign, source, medium, creative_id |
| `order_click` | Visitor clicked from landing page to Toast | campaign, placement, creative_id |
| `chooser_select` | Visitor made a product choice | protein, format |
| `promo_redeem` | Completed order used the campaign code | code, order value, new/repeat customer if available |

### Weekly scorecard

Track per creative and roll up by content set:

- Reach and 3-second views.
- Average watch time and completion rate.
- Shares and saves per 1,000 reached.
- Profile visits.
- Landing-page sessions.
- Order clicks.
- Landing-page-to-order-click rate.
- Promo-code redemptions or another completed-order proxy.
- Revenue and average order value when available.

### Decision rules

- Do not select a winner on views alone.
- Require at least four posts per set before judging the message.
- The winning set is the one with the best completed-order rate. If order data is unavailable, use order-click rate as the temporary proxy and label it honestly.
- If one set wins attention while another wins order clicks, use the first as the hook and the second as the landing-page/message architecture in month two.
- If all three pages receive traffic but few order clicks, the problem is offer, menu clarity or price—not content reach.
- If order clicks are strong but validated orders are weak, audit Toast handoff, fees, unavailable items and checkout abandonment.

## Owner inputs required before public launch

1. Confirm the preferred direct ordering platform and whether Toast can report campaign attribution.
2. Confirm current delivery radius, operating hours and item availability.
3. Approve or reject a first-order incentive; do not publish an offer until it exists in the POS.
4. Grant access to Instagram Insights and any available Toast/Elevate reporting.
5. Supply the current logo file and highest-resolution original food photos.
6. Approve use of staff, student, athlete and customer likenesses for organic content and landing pages.
7. Identify the owner of weekly posting, comment replies and customer-service escalation.

## First seven days

1. Install analytics and verify all three landing-page order-click events.
2. Clean the direct Toast menu and confirm exact deep links.
3. Film the shared shot list and the first three hero videos.
4. Publish one post from each set with matching landing-page destinations.
5. Review results after seven days without declaring a final winner.
6. Adjust hooks and editing while preserving the underlying message test.
